How to read this chart

  • Black line — estimated caffeine concentration over time from your logged intakes.
  • Shaded band — uncertainty range (dose content ±20% and fast vs slow metabolizer bounds).
  • Vertical bedtime marker — your selected bedtime.
  • Dashed Baur line — published research reference from Baur et al. (2024), not a personal sleep cutoff.

Colored zones (pharmacokinetic bands)

  • Low (<0.5 µg/mL) — Lower estimated residual caffeine.
  • Lower (0.5–1.0 µg/mL) — Small effects possible in sensitive individuals.
  • Elevated (1.0–1.4 µg/mL) — Measurable sleep effects more common in research.
  • High (1.4–2.5 µg/mL) — Includes approximate Baur delta-power reference region.
  • Very high (>2.5 µg/mL) — Higher likelihood of substantial sleep disruption.

Sleep disruption zone reference
About these zones: Thresholds are derived from pharmacokinetic and sleep research and represent approximate population averages. Individual sensitivity varies considerably. The zones reflect estimated probability of sleep impact, not guarantees. All concentrations are plasma estimates using a one-compartment model.
🟢 Low estimated level
< 0.5 µg/mL
Adenosine receptors likely unoccupied. In Baur et al. (2024), minimal EEG delta-power changes were reported at lower plasma levels. Sleep onset and architecture are unlikely to be meaningfully affected for most people.
🟡 Moderate estimated level
0.5–1.0 µg/mL
Some adenosine receptor blockade may persist. Population studies (e.g. Gardiner et al. 2023 meta-analysis) report average dose-dependent effects on sleep latency and total sleep time; sensitive individuals may notice milder sleep onset.
🟠 Elevated estimated level
1.0–1.4 µg/mL
Significant receptor blockade likely. Research suggests measurable average effects on sleep onset and quality at this level, approaching the Baur delta-power reference. Individual responses vary.
🔴 High estimated level
1.4–2.5 µg/mL (at/above Baur delta-power reference ~1.4 µg/mL)
High estimated caffeine level. Baur et al. (2024) associated concentrations around and above ~1.4 µg/mL with reduced EEG delta power in their controlled protocol. Group averages also suggest delayed sleep onset and reduced sleep quality when caffeine is taken closer to bed.
⛔ Very high estimated level
> 2.5 µg/mL
Very high levels are likely to delay sleep onset and reduce total sleep time in population averages. Individual variation is real, but research consistently shows stronger average effects on sleep architecture across this concentration range.

What is the Baur line?

In Baur et al. (2024), researchers measured plasma caffeine and EEG during controlled sleep recordings. Around ~1.4 µg/mL, they reported measurable reduction in delta power—a marker of deep slow-wave sleep intensity in that lab protocol.

The dashed line on the caffeine curve marks that study finding. Broader dose–timing effects on sleep are supported by other studies, which do not use the same plasma threshold. It is not a rule for whether you personally will sleep well tonight.

Model limitations: This calculator uses a one-compartment pharmacokinetic model with simplified half-life modifiers for CYP1A2 metabolizer type, oral contraceptives (~70% longer), smoking (~50% shorter), and pregnancy (~2× longer). When multiple modifiers are selected, estimates are less precise. It does not account for tolerance, trimester-specific pregnancy changes, individual EEG, or circadian phase. Output is for educational planning—not medical advice or a guarantee of how you will sleep tonight.
What changes half-life in this model:
  • CYP1A2 metabolizer type — fast (~−12.5%), average, or slow (~+37.5%)
  • Oral contraceptives — ~70% longer half-life (×1.70)
  • Smoking — ~50% faster clearance (×0.5 on half-life)
  • Pregnancy — ~2× longer half-life (simplified; real values vary by trimester)
  • Body weight & food — affect peak timing/height, not half-life

Uncertainty: Two or more hormonal/lifestyle factors together (especially smoking + pregnancy, or all three with oral contraceptives) produce less predictable clearance. Discuss caffeine use with a healthcare provider if any apply.

Important health considerations

You selected smoking, pregnancy, and oral contraceptives. This tool provides approximate caffeine clearance estimates only—it is not medical advice.

  • Smoking while using oral contraceptives is associated with increased cardiovascular risk.
  • Smoking during pregnancy is harmful to the baby.
  • Combined effects on caffeine metabolism are complex and less studied; half-life estimates here are less precise.

Please discuss caffeine intake, smoking, and contraception with a qualified healthcare provider.
