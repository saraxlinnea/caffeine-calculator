Sleep Disruption Zones

Sleep disruption thresholds: Based on peer-reviewed research. Each zone shows what happens to your sleep at different caffeine plasma concentrations.
🟢 SAFE
< 0.5 µg/mL
Sleep impact: Minimal to none • REM: Normal • Deep sleep: Unaffected
🟡 CAUTION
0.5–1.0 µg/mL
Sleep impact: Subtle effects • REM: Latency +10–20 min • Deep sleep: Slightly reduced
🟠 WARNING
1.0–1.4 µg/mL
Sleep impact: REM disruption • REM: Latency +30–60 min • Deep sleep: 20–40% reduced
🔴 NOT RECOMMENDED
1.4–2.5 µg/mL
Sleep impact: Significant disruption • REM: Severely delayed • Deep sleep: 50%+ reduced
⛔ AVOID
> 2.5 µg/mL
Sleep impact: Severe insomnia-like symptoms • REM: Extreme latency • Deep sleep: Nearly eliminated

Full Citations & Sources

Sleep Disruption Thresholds
Baur et al. (2023)
"Concentration-effect relationships of plasma caffeine on EEG delta power and cardiac autonomic activity during human sleep" — establishes the 1.4 µg/mL threshold for measurable deep-sleep reduction.
medRxiv: 2023.10.14.23297036v1
Gardiner et al. (2024)
"Caffeine and sleep in adults: A systematic review of observational studies" — reviews REM latency changes across plasma concentration ranges.
PubMed: PMID 38733154
Weibel et al. (2021)
"Effects of caffeine on the human circadian clock in vivo and in vitro" — documents fragmented sleep architecture above 1.4 µg/mL.
Science Translational Medicine: eabd6437
Pharmacokinetics & Half-Life
Liguori et al. (1997)
"Absorption and subjective effects of caffeine from coffee, cola and capsules" — Tmax values (45 min fasting baseline) and bioavailability (~99%).
PubMed: PMID 9051820
NCBI Bookshelf
"Caffeine Pharmacology" — volume of distribution (0.6 L/kg), clearance rates, and peak concentration modeling.
NCBI: NBK223808
Arnaud (1987)
"The pharmacokinetics of caffeine and its metabolites" — half-life range 2.5–10 hours with mean ~5 hours in healthy adults.
PubMed: PMID 3304513
Sex & Hormonal Factors
Abernethy & Todd (1985)
"Impairment of caffeine clearance by chronic use of low-dose estrogen-containing oral contraceptives" — oral contraceptives extend half-life by ~30%.
PubMed: PMID 4070084
Gu et al. (1992)
"Biotransformation of caffeine and theophylline in healthy volunteers" — documents sex-based differences in CYP1A2 activity (+12.5% longer half-life in females).
PubMed: PMID 1632833
Genetics (CYP1A2)
Cornelis et al. (2011)
"Genome-wide meta-analysis identifies regions on 7p21 (AHR) and 15q24 (CYP1A2) as determinants of habitual caffeine consumption" — fast/slow metabolizer genotype frequencies and effect sizes.
PubMed Central: PMC3072367
Sachse et al. (1999)
"Functional significance of a C→A polymorphism in intron 1 of the cytochrome P450 CYP1A2 gene tested with caffeine" — AA genotype clears caffeine 25% faster than CC genotype.
PubMed: PMID 9862246
Food & Absorption
Gastroenterology (2019)
"Emptying and Absorption of Caffeine from the Human Stomach" — food-dependent Tmax delays: light meal +15 min, moderate meal +30 min, heavy/high-fat meal +60 min.
MDPI Pharmaceutics (2024)
"Intragastric factors and absorption kinetics of caffeine" — gastric emptying rate as a primary determinant of caffeine absorption speed; peak height unchanged by food.
MDPI: Pharmaceutics 16(1), 33
